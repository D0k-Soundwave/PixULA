## Presets

There are two preset libraries. They differ in how much of the app they are
allowed to change.

**A tool preset** holds one panel's settings under a name. Save and load it from
the row at the foot of that panel, or from the Presets panel in the sidebar. It
changes that panel and nothing else - not your colours, not the draw mode, not
the view. You reach for these while drawing, so a control that also moved the
canvas or swapped your reference photo would be one you could not use with any
confidence.

The Presets panel lists every tool's presets together, so its rows lead with the
tool and a summary of what the preset changes, with the name you gave it on
hover. The row under a tool's own options lists only that tool's presets, where
the name is enough to tell them apart.

**A workspace preset** holds the whole setup in one of nine slots. Recall one
with Alt and its number, and manage them from **Settings > Workspace Presets**.
There are nine because there are nine keys within reach - a slot you cannot get
to with a chord is a slot you would not use mid-picture.

A workspace preset stores only the parts you tick when saving it, and restores
only those.

Two things are deliberately not in that list.

**Screen mode**, because switching it converts your artwork and can lose detail,
which is not something a preset should do without asking. A preset records which
mode it was saved in and skips its colours if you recall it somewhere the
palette would mean something different.

**The palette**, because a palette is a file you build once and reuse across
pictures. Load and save it directly instead - see the colour chapter.
