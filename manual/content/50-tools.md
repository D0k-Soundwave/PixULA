## The tools

The rail groups tools by what they do to the picture: first everything that
lays down ink, then selection, then the guide that selection powers, then the
tools that place finished artwork, then the ones that only change your view.

The draw mode, the mirror setting and the drawing guide apply to every tool
here, so they are set once in the colour bar instead of appearing in every
tool's options.

![The tool options panel showing the brush settings](img/tool-options-brush.png)

*The options for the selected tool appear in this panel - here, the brush.*

The brush has no crosshair over the picture. At size 2 and up, the outline of
the area it will paint is the pointer. At size 1 the pointer is a small plus
three pixels across, and the dot in its middle is the pixel the brush will
paint, in the colour it will paint: your ink with Bright applied, or the cell's
own ink when Ink is set to use the existing colour. While you draw, the dot
shows what you have just painted. Over the grey area around the picture the
normal pointer comes back. The Spray, Fade, Pattern and Hatch tools work the
same way.

Many options only apply in combination with another. The tables below have a
**Shown when** column giving the condition under which each row appears; rows
marked "always" are there whatever else is set.

**Point figures.** The Shape list has a group of Figures, drawn by placing points: Polyline, Polygon, Triangle, Rays, Perpendicular line and Parallel line (the L key picks the Figure tool). Click to add a point, or press and drag for a freehand run. A double click or Enter finishes the figure, which stays on the picture as a preview you can still change; Enter again draws it, as one undo step. Backspace removes the last point (a whole freehand run at a time), Escape drops the figure without drawing it, and clicking elsewhere or choosing another tool draws it. The Triangle, Perpendicular line and Parallel line finish by themselves at their third point: drag out the first line, then click the last point. A Perpendicular line adds a second line from the foot of the perpendicular from your point to the first line; a Parallel line adds a copy of the first line with your point at its middle. Rays joins every later point to the first. Polygon and Triangle can be filled. Figures follow the draw mode, Inverse, the "use existing" boxes, mirroring and the drawing guide like any shape, and in the preview exactly as they will be drawn. The Parallelogram also finishes by itself at its third point: drag out the first side, then click the corner after the second one; the fourth corner is worked out so the shape is always a parallelogram, and it can be filled.

**Line style, Rotation and Rounding.** The Shape tool, the Figure tool and the Bezier curve share a Line style: Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Dash-dot, Dash-dot-dot and Clear. The dash styles are drawn on a one-pixel line only, so they are greyed out above a thickness of 1; the pattern runs along the outline, round the corners, and each ray or line of a figure starts its own. Clear draws no outline, so use it with Filled to draw just the inside. The Bezier curve has no Clear. Rotation (-180 to 180 degrees, clockwise) turns a rectangle, square, rounded rectangle, ellipse or parallelogram round the middle of its box, and turns an arc, sector, triangle, diamond or regular polygon round the point you pressed. Rounding sets how round the corners of the rounded rectangle are, from square corners at 0 to half-circle ends at 100. A Bezier curve you are still editing is drawn when you pick another tool, and Escape drops it. The preview of a shape or curve shows the mirrored copies and keeps inside the drawing guide, as the finished drawing will.

**Editing, moving, turning and cloning a figure.** A finished Figure, and a Bezier curve, stay on the picture as a preview until you draw them. While one is waiting you can drag any of its square points to reshape it, drag the figure itself to move it, drag the round knob above it to turn it, and use the arrow keys to move it (one pixel, one cell with Shift, or one snap step with grid snap). Ctrl+Z takes back the last of these edits, then the figure itself, and only then the picture. Enter, a click elsewhere, saving or choosing another tool draws it, as one undo step. A shape from the Shape tool normally draws as soon as you let go; tick **Keep editable** in its options and it waits like a figure, with a handle on each end of the drag. **Edit > Clone Last Figure** (Shift+C) opens a copy of the last figure, shape or curve you drew, ready to move, turn or change; to change the colours or line style of one you have already drawn, press Ctrl+Z and then Clone Last Figure. While a figure is waiting it follows the colours, line style and fill you choose, as the finished drawing will. A Bezier curve is the exception: you reshape it by its handles and move it with the arrow keys, but it cannot be dragged whole or turned, and Ctrl+Z drops the whole curve at once.

**Spray Pattern.** Set the Spray tool's Distribution to **Pattern** and it lays small whole figures - circles, squares or short lines - instead of dots. Choose the figure, its size and whether it is filled or an outline; Flow sets how much of the area they try to cover. Only whole figures are laid inside the spray area, so a spray too small for one figure draws nothing.

**Snap and its step.** Snap (the button under the canvas, or Shift+S) makes selections, shapes, figure points, text and pasted pieces land on a grid; freehand drawing is never snapped. The list beside the button sets the grid: the attribute cell of the mode (8x8, or 8x4, 8x2 or 8x1 in the multicolor modes), 16x16, a whole 256x192 screen (only on a canvas bigger than one screen, such as Layer 2 320 and 640), or your own step, whose across and down sizes you set in Preferences under Drawing. Choosing a step also turns Snap on. A pasted piece lands with its top-left corner on the grid. With Snap on, the arrow keys move a waiting figure, a floating piece or the gradient's end by one step; a selection that carries its colours moves by the step rounded to whole cells. ZX Paintbrush moves things pixel by pixel with the keyboard whatever its raster jump is, so this differs on purpose.

**Selection sizes.** With the rectangle selection, the **Size** list chooses what a drag selects. Free is a rectangle of any size. Cell selects the whole attribute cells it touches. 16x16, Screen (256x192, on a canvas bigger than one screen) and Custom (a block width and height you set) select whole blocks of that size: a click picks one block, a drag picks every block it reaches. Cell blocks sit on the cell grid; the other blocks start where you press the mouse, or, with Snap on, on the snap cell you pressed in. A block never crosses the edge of the picture. With Snap on, a Free drag from one snap point to another selects exactly the distance between them.

**Where the click goes, shadows and background.** **Anchor** chooses which point of the finished text block the click is: any of the four corners, the middle of an edge, or the centre (the default, where text has always landed). It is the finished block that is anchored - after the outline, the shadow and the Direction turn - so with a turned text "Top left" is the top-left of the turned block. With Snap on, the block's top-left corner lands on the grid. **Shadow direction** (shown while Shadow is ticked) sets whether the shadow falls down-right, down-left, up-right or up-left; the direction belongs to the letters, so it turns with Direction, and a Mirror does not flip it. **Shadow dots** and **Contour dots** choose how each effect is drawn: Same as letters (the original) or Opposite, which is how ZX Paintbrush shades text - the letters keep the dots the draw mode gives them and the shade or the ring around them is drawn in the opposite state, a cleared shade behind set letters, or with Inverse a set shade behind reset letters. Opposite dots are not drawn in XOR, Ink only or Paper only (they have no opposite), and a hint says so. **Background** set to Clear background also clears the dots of the whole text box (the Transform panel's Clear dots, kept in step); with it an opposite shade merges into the cleared box. Right-button stamping ignores Background and the opposite dots.

**Type on canvas.** Tick Type on canvas in the Text tool (ZX ROM or a saved font only) to type straight onto the picture, as in ZX Paintbrush. Click where the text starts - the click is the top-left of the first letter, snapped to the grid when Snap is on - and type. A flashing caret shows where the next letter goes; Left, Right, Up, Down, Home and End move it, Backspace and Delete remove letters, and Shift+Enter starts a new line. Character width and Character height stretch the font to any size from 1 to 64 dots, and Character spacing and Line spacing (-8 to 32) open the text out. Direction, mirror, bold, italic, shadow, contour, the opposite dots and Background all apply live. While you type, the picture and the Undo list are untouched: Enter (or the Draw button) draws the whole text in one Undo step, Escape (or Cancel) throws it away, and clicking elsewhere on the picture, choosing another tool or saving draws it first; New and Open drop it. A character the font does not have is refused with a notice. The shortcut keys are off while you type. On a touch device the on-screen keyboard is used, and the Draw, New line and Cancel buttons stand in for Enter, Shift+Enter and Escape. Touching an option does not interrupt typing: the next key still goes into the text. ZX Paintbrush's flashing L cursor is a plain caret here. With a big or proportional saved font (see the Font Editor) each letter is as wide as it is saved, Character width is the width of a full-size letter (narrower letters keep their proportion), and the Size list goes up in steps of the font's own height. Changing to a font of another height keeps your zoom: ROM stretched to 16 high becomes a 16 high font at 32.
